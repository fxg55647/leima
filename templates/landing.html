Evidence, packed and sealed

When “trust me, bro” isn’t enough.

Collect evidence. Seal it permanently. Let others check.

Keep the sources, original files and observations behind your claims together in a verifiable evidence package.

Keep the package. Share the evidence. Let others inspect it.

Everyone deserves a way to back up their word.

In a world of convincing fakes and uncertain claims, being believed should not depend on who has the loudest voice. Everyone should be able to present evidence — and everyone’s claims should be open to the same scrutiny.

Leima helps you collect and preserve evidence of what you saw, what you made and what you were told. A permanent seal gives others a way to check that the evidence matches the record you created.

What you can build evidence for

Give your claims something to stand on.

01 / Sources

Verifiable source citations

Connect a claim to its source and the exact passage you rely on. Preserve the source alongside the citation so others can inspect the evidence.

For research, journalism, reports and AI-generated answers.

Explore a source citation →
02 / Origins

Art provenance

Give your work a documented history. Collect sketches, original files and records of its creation as evidence of where it came from.

For artists, collectors and anyone documenting a work’s origins.

Work with an evidence bundle →
03 / People

Evidence of human work

Show the process behind the result. Preserve drafts, work in progress and supporting observations as evidence of your contribution.

For creators and students. Evidence of a process helps reviewers assess human involvement; it does not certify the absence of AI.

Bring your supporting evidence →
04 / Capture

Photos, video & sensor evidence

Build a record around visual evidence, with GPS location, motion, orientation and other available sensor data providing context for the capture.

The Android prototype currently collects photos, optional GPS and sensor logs. Video capture is not offered in this workflow yet.

Explore the field capture prototype →
05 / The web

Web evidence

Preserve what a website showed. Keep captured content, its source URL and available collection details so others can examine the material behind your claim.

For published promises, public statements and content that may change.

Start with a webpage →
06 / Correspondence

Email provenance

Preserve original emails with their authentication results. Let others inspect the message and the available evidence of its sending domain and signed content.

For agreements, commitments and correspondence you may need to substantiate.

Illustrative example / Source citation

A source you can actually inspect.

A citation should let the reader follow the claim back to the evidence. Open each part of this fictional example.

The claim and its source

Claim: “The product reduces every customer’s energy consumption by 18%.”

“Participating customers in the pilot reduced their energy consumption by an average of 18%.”

Example source: a fictional pilot report. A real package would include the source material and its recorded provenance.

What the evidence supports

The passage reports an average among pilot participants. It does not establish the same reduction for every customer. A narrower claim would be: “Pilot participants reduced their energy consumption by an average of 18%.”

What the seal lets you check

For a real sealed package, the verifier compares the files with the permanently recorded fingerprints. That checks whether the material matches the sealed version. Readers can separately assess whether the source and interpretation are convincing.

This example explains the workflow; it is not a stamped report.

Preserve the evidence. Leave room for judgment.

People and AI tools can examine the same material, challenge conclusions and make their own assessments. The strength of a claim depends on the evidence, its origin and how it was collected.

AI answers need source citations too.

Give AI-generated claims an inspectable foundation. Keep the source alongside an assessment of whether it supports the claim, so readers can review the evidence themselves.

Check a claim against a source →

A permanent record — what does that mean?

Leima records the cryptographic fingerprints of your evidence package on Arweave, a network designed for permanent data storage. These fingerprints let someone check whether the files they hold match the sealed record.

Is it really forever?

Permanent storage is the network’s design goal, not an unconditional guarantee of eternity. Your original files stay in your evidence package, so keep a safe copy. The seal allows those files to be checked; it cannot recover them if they are lost.
